## Building an Admin Panel from Scratch (A New Experience)

One day, while I was busy tweaking my terminal theme, one of my former clients reached out again:  
*"I need an admin panel, but without any frameworks. Just deliver the raw pages, and I’ll handle server-side rendering myself."*

I was used to building projects like this with tools such as <a href='https://nextjs.org/' target='_blank'>Next.js</a>.  
But this time was different.  
That’s when I realized I was about to dive into a **completely new experience**—  
because I had never built such a relatively large project **without helper tools** and entirely **from scratch**.  

Since I love new challenges, I decided to take it on.

---

## 👨🏻‍💻 What I Did

I spent about two to three days figuring out the right architecture—because in my opinion, a well-structured architecture makes a project far more maintainable and scalable in the long run.  

- With <a href='https://tailwindcss.com/' target='_blank'>TailwindCSS</a>, I set up the design foundation: colors, typography, spacing, and more.  
- For interactive parts, I went with <a href='https://jquery.com/' target='_blank'>jQuery</a> to save time and avoid reinventing the wheel where possible.  
- To improve **reusability** and enhance the <a href='https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developer_Experience' target='_blank'>DX</a>, I used <a href='https://handlebarsjs.com/' target='_blank'>Handlebars</a>.  
- For bundling, I chose <a href='https://vite.dev/' target='_blank'>Vite</a> and spent a few hours setting it up for both development and production builds.  

Once the basics were ready, I started building components piece by piece.  
The pages slowly came together like a puzzle until the entire panel was complete.

---

## ⚡ Optimization & Performance

To make sure the panel loads fast and consumes minimal resources, I focused on:

- Using semantic and proper HTML tags  
- Removing unused CSS  
- Compressing images  
- Cleaning up unnecessary imports  

---

## 🔥 The Final Result

The outcome was exactly what I hoped for:  
A clean, maintainable admin panel that is both **fast** and **optimized**.
