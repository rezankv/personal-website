## How It Started


For a while, I had been thinking about finding a better way to present my portfolio. Writing about my projects in chats or simply sending demo links never felt appealing to me. I wanted a dedicated space where I could share everything about my work in a more customized way—images, videos, text, and even voice notes.

I had also seen a few personal websites from foreign developers that I really liked, and that inspired me to create something similar for myself. So I decided to build my own personal website, a place where I can showcase my projects exactly the way I want.

## How It Went

After that, I started analyzing what requirements I needed to think about. I wanted the website to be fast, the design to stay minimal, and I didn’t want to deal with databases, servers, or anything overly complicated.

So I kept searching until I figured out the right approach. For the design part, I browsed Twitter and checked other developers’ portfolio websites to gather inspiration.

To maintain the highest possible performance, I decided to make the website fully static. This means no external data sources — all the content lives directly inside the codebase. If you look through the source code, you’ll see how I structured everything. I might even write a blog post about it later.

In the end, I deployed everything on GitHub, and that was it.

## Key Features
- Dark and Light Themes  
- Bilingual Support  
- Fully Static Content Delivery  
- High Performance  
- Minimal Design
